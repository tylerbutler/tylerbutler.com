title: "Fun with Magnets!"

---

For most of my adult life I have been depressed. Over the past few years, as my family and I have endured a seemingly
endless cavalcade of pain and suffering, my depression has gotten steadily worse. My sister’s death, our infertility,
the loss of my cousin to cancer… it all takes its mental and emotional toll. My depression was recently supplemented by a
new diagnosis -- prolonged grief disorder. The Butlers have always been pioneers in various ways so I suppose I’m proud
to be the first Butler to be sporting a DSM-V-acknowledged diagnosis.

I have not been alone in my fight against depression. My wife and family are with me. I have good mental health benefits which I take advantage of. I see a therapist. I also take medication, and the amount of meds has grown over the years as the fight has grown more dire. Sometimes it feels like I have to work incredibly hard just to stay afloat, let alone make any positive progress.

I took three months of leave last year to try and get on top of it, and it helped. But I’m still not in good shape.

Which is what brought me to another treatment for depression which sounds like hogwash but is real: treating depression
with high-powered magnets. That’s basically what [repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (rTMS)][tms] is. They
shoot high-powered magnetic pulses into my brain, where they induce a small electric current and help form new neural
pathways. Pathways which, if all goes to plan, will help my brain better cope with the realities of depression. My goal
is not to get free of medication completely, though that would of course be welcome. Rather, I just hope to reduce the
need for so many meds, or raise the effectiveness of the meds.

The science behind TMS is pretty cool, and you can read more about it, but what you might not be able to find is a description of what the treatments are like. That’s what I hope this helps with. And note that I did say _treatments_. This is not a one time thing. TMS is 8 weeks of daily 20-minute treatments at an office in Seattle. With the commute it’s about 80-90 minutes of commitment, every weekday. It’s a lot.

Fortunately the treatments aren’t uncomfortable for the most part. I don't have to be strapped down or anything, but I
do have to wear a skullcap that makes me look a little like a Mercury astronaut. Then the machine fits over my head and
I get strapped in.

It's more annoying to take off my glasses for 20 minutes than the treatments themselves.

[tms]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcranial_magnetic_stimulation
